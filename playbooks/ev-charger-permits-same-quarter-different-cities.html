EV-charger permits, Q3 2026 vs Q3 2025: how to read them city by city

Five places, one permit type, the same quarter a year apart. Each city is compared only with its own past, because cities count and date permits differently. Here is how to read the numbers without over-reading them.

The whole path at a glance

Step by step

  1. Know what the count is and isn't

    • The count is permits whose record mentions EV chargers or charging stations.
    • It is not the number of chargers installed. One permit can cover many chargers, and some records don't use the words.
    Watch out: A permit can sit in more than one category, so category counts don't add up to a city total.
    Sources

    Picker's Desk Trends methodology

  2. Use counts only where there are enough of them

    • With 20 or more permits in the earlier quarter, we show a percentage. With fewer, we show the two counts and nothing more.
    • Chicago: 116 to 100 (-14%). Austin, commercial permits only: 32 to 16 (-50%, we don't know why yet).
    • Too few to compare: Marin County 19 to 41, Campbell 15 to 24, San Francisco 14 to 36 (applications filed).
    Watch out: Small counts swing widely. A rise from 14 to 36 is real in the records, but it is not a trend.
    Sources

    Picker's Desk Trends methodology · City of Chicago data portal

  3. Check which date each city counts

    • Most places are counted by the date the permit was issued. San Francisco is counted by the date the application was filed, and the Trends page labels it that way.
    • A filed date and an issued date are different moments, so do not set one city's number beside another's.
    Sources

    Picker's Desk Trends methodology · City of Austin open data: issued construction permits

  4. Read the change, then stop

    • Say what the records show and no more. A fall in one city's EV-charger permits does not say why, and it says nothing about another city.
    • When a move is large and the reason is unknown, say "we don't know why yet." Our companion playbook walks through the checks.
    Sources

    Picker's Desk Trends methodology

Honest effort and what goes wrong

About twenty minutes if you start from the Trends page and check one city's data notes. The comparison itself is quick; the work is making sure the quarters, dates and counts line up.

  • Setting one city's count beside another's.
  • Turning a small count into a percentage.
  • Reading a permit count as a number of chargers.
  • Guessing a reason for a move the records don't explain.
