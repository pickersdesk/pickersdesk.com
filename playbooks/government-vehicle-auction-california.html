Buy a work vehicle at a government auction in California

State, federal, county and city fleets sell used trucks and vans online. Here's the path from finding a sale to driving it legally: the sellers, the paperwork, the DMV steps, and the ways it goes wrong.

The whole path at a glance

Step by step

  1. Pick a seller and register where it sells

    • The State of California (Department of General Services) sells state fleet vehicles online through Bar None Auction, monthly, and lets you view them at its Sacramento, Fresno and Fontana yards in the two days before each auction.
    • Federal vehicles, mostly 3 to 5 years old and coming off lease, sell through GSA Fleet Auctions. Anyone 18 or older can bid, and GSA says it charges no buyer's fees.
    • Counties and cities use different platforms: Marin and Contra Costa list on Public Surplus, Santa Cruz on GovDeals, Placer on both, and the City of Sacramento on Bar None. Your own county or city's surplus page names its platform; register there before the sale.
    Watch out: Only bid through the platform the agency's own page names; look-alike sites exist.

  2. Read both sets of terms before you bid

    • The State sells every vehicle as is, where is: no warranty, repairs, refunds or exchanges, and bidding means you accept those terms.
    • GSA promises only that the vehicle matches its description. A misdescription claim has to be filed within 15 days of removal and can't recover more than you paid.
    • Each platform adds its own rules for buyer's premium, payment, pickup and storage. Read them on the listing before you bid; they change by seller.
    Watch out: None of the official pages we checked offers test drives. Assume you can't drive it before you buy.
    Sources

    CA DGS State Surplus · GSA Fleet sale terms (PDF)

  3. Check the paperwork it comes with

    • State vehicles sold with documents come with a California certificate of title. Vehicles sold without documents have to be towed off the lot, and DMV issues a salvage title.
    • Federal vehicles come with an SF-97 and a purchaser's receipt instead of a title. The SF-97 is issued only in the buyer's name, and salvage vehicles get a branded one.
    • A city or county vehicle sold in working order comes with its title and registration card. One sold because repairs cost more than it's worth comes with a bill of sale and a smog notice, and is re-registered as a revived junk vehicle.
    Watch out: A salvage or junk vehicle needs a DMV or CHP inspection and official brake and lamp certificates before it can be registered.
    Sources

    CA DGS State Surplus · GSA Fleet sale terms (PDF) · DMV: vehicles sold by a public agency · DMV: vehicle verifications

  4. Diesel truck? Check the engine year first

    • Since January 1, 2023, nearly every diesel truck over 14,000 lb gross vehicle weight that operates in California must have a 2010 or newer engine.
    • DMV checks this before registering the truck. A truck that doesn't comply gets only a 90-day temporary operating permit, and CARB says it can't extend it.

  5. Inspect it, then set a top price that includes everything

    • Go to the preview if there is one. State vehicles can only be seen in the two days before each auction.
    • Your top price is the bid plus the platform's buyer's premium, towing or transport, any storage, use tax, registration and smog, and the repairs you saw. Decide it before the auction starts and don't go past it.
    Sources

    CA DGS State Surplus · DMV registration fees and calculator · CDTFA: use tax on vehicles

  6. Win, pay and pick up on time

    • GSA requires payment within 2 business days of the award email and removal within 10 calendar days, with no extensions. Missing either is a default: you lose the vehicle and owe damages, up to $875 per lot.
    • State vehicles bought through Bar None are picked up the week after the auction, by appointment on weekdays, at your cost and risk. GSA doesn't arrange transport either.
    Watch out: Line up a tow or a driver, insurance and a temporary permit before the auction ends, not after.
    Sources

    GSA Fleet sale terms (PDF) · CA DGS State Surplus

  7. Register it with DMV within 10 days

    • Report the change of ownership to DMV within 10 days. With a title in hand, it's a standard transfer; if the title is missing, a REG 227 comes first.
    • Ex-state or local government vehicles had exempt plates: those are surrendered, you get regular plates, and you pay full-year registration fees. State-owned vehicles also need a transfer permit from the selling department.
    • Federal SF-97 vehicles register like a new original registration: a REG 343 application, a vehicle verification, the SF-97 and the odometer disclosure, plus weight papers for commercial trucks. These go to DMV headquarters, so expect them to take longer.
    • DMV collects use tax at the rate for your address, unless an exemption applies. A federal sale is exempt only if the SF-97 cites the specific code the DMV manual lists.
    • Smog: most gas vehicles need a passing check to transfer; vehicles under four model years old pay a fee instead; diesels from 1997 or earlier or over 14,000 lb are exempt. Vehicles of 10,001 lb or more register as commercial.

Honest effort and what goes wrong

Expect a few hours per vehicle: watching listings, one preview trip, reading two sets of terms, a pickup trip, and a DMV visit that can run longer for federal vehicles. Many bidders lose more auctions than they win, and that's fine: walking away above your top price is the plan working.

  • The vehicle needs more work than it looked like, and there's no refund.
  • It's sold without documents: salvage title, a tow, inspections and brake and lamp certificates before it can drive.
  • A diesel truck with a pre-2010 engine can't be registered for regular use in California.
  • Payment or pickup is late: the vehicle is lost and damages are owed.
  • It fails smog and can't be registered until it passes.
  • The extras (premium, tow, storage, use tax, registration, repairs) add up to more than a dealer price.
