Which Bay Area cities publish building-permit data? We checked 45.

If you sell to builders or do commercial trade work, building permits are one of the earliest public signs of a job. But in the Bay Area, whether you can actually get that data depends on which side of a city line the job is on. On October 16, 2026 we checked 45 cities and counties. Four publish current permit data you can download.

How we checked

We only counted records published by the government agency itself: its own open data portal, its own map server, or its official permit-system feed. We didn't count third-party permit sites or resellers. For each place we looked through those sources, searched the web, and opened every candidate dataset to see how recent its newest record was. A dataset counted as current if it had permits filed or issued in the last 30 days, with an address, a date and a description, and if it included commercial work.

This is a snapshot of one day. Agencies switch systems, and feeds start and stop. “Not found” means we didn't find it, not that it can't exist. If your city publishes something we missed, please tell me at roger@pickersdesk.com and I'll update this page.

Current, downloadable permit data (4)

Updated at least weekly, straight from the agency, with addresses, dates and descriptions for commercial work.

  • San Francisco. Building permits on DataSF, updated daily. Estimated cost, use and description; no contractor.
  • San José. Active building permits on the city's open data portal, updated daily. Valuation and contractor included.
  • Marin County (unincorporated areas). “Building Permit” dataset on the county's open data portal, updated daily. Valuation, and contractor on over-the-counter permits.
  • Campbell. Permit layer on the city's ArcGIS site, newest records two to three days old when we checked. Descriptions and dates; no valuation or contractor.

Current, but missing a piece we need (3)

Fresh records that leave out something basic, like the street address.

  • Santa Clara County (unincorporated areas). Current development records with descriptions, but only a parcel number, not a street address, and only a status date.
  • City of San Mateo. Current planning applications (not building permits) on the city's ArcGIS site.
  • Napa County. A current list of about 90 planning projects (wineries, use permits), not building permits.

Published as PDF or spreadsheet reports (5)

Official and useful to read, but not a data feed. Each report has to be downloaded and taken apart by hand.

  • Los Gatos. Monthly “Permit Activity – Issued” PDF; September 2026 was posted when we checked. Valuation, contractor and description included.
  • Sonoma County (Permit Sonoma). Monthly spreadsheets of permit applications, posted around the middle of the following month. Its open data feed stopped at May 30, 2025.
  • Contra Costa County (unincorporated areas). Weekly “Building Permits Issued” PDFs with valuation and contractor. The county's website blocked our automated downloads, so we couldn't confirm the newest one.
  • Fairfield. Issued-permit PDF reports; the newest we found covered February 2026.
  • Vallejo. Monthly permit PDFs; the newest listed was December 2018.

Had a permit dataset, but it stopped updating (11)

The dataset is still online (or was until recently) but has no recent records. Several of these portals still show a recent “updated” date even though the records inside stopped.

  • Milpitas. Rich permit data on the city's ArcGIS site, newest records August 4, 2026.
  • Palo Alto. The city's Accela feed on civicdata.com was last updated around July 1, 2026 and no longer loads. The city's newer permit map shows addresses only.
  • Alameda. Accela feed on civicdata.com last updated around July 1, 2026; the data no longer loads.
  • Concord. Accela feed on civicdata.com last updated around June 30, 2026; the data no longer loads.
  • Sonoma County. Construction permits on the county's open data portal: newest records May 30, 2025.
  • Richmond. PermitTrak on the city's open data portal: newest applications April 2025.
  • Sunnyvale. Permit history on the city's map server: newest records January 2024, and no addresses.
  • Walnut Creek. Permit metrics on civicdata.com through April 2024. The city's website blocked our checks.
  • Berkeley. New building permits on the city's ArcGIS site through June 2021.
  • Santa Rosa. Building permits on the city's ArcGIS site through June 2018.
  • San Mateo County. Housing permit layers (residential only) last edited in 2018.

Residential only, or behind a login (3)

Something is published, but not commercial work, or not to the public.

  • Fremont. Major residential projects only (2021).
  • Dublin. Accessory dwelling unit (ADU) permits only.
  • Burlingame. The permit map service on the city's server requires a login.

What stood out

  • Feeds stop quietly. Sonoma County's open data portal still shows a recent update date, but the newest permit in it is from May 30, 2025. Alameda, Concord and Palo Alto's feeds on civicdata.com still appear in search but no longer load. If you rely on one of these, check the dates inside the data, not the “updated” label.
  • PDF reports are still common. Los Gatos and Contra Costa County post detailed issued-permit reports, often with valuation and contractor. They're official and useful to read, just not easy to work with in bulk.
  • Look-alike names. Two ArcGIS sites that come up for “Hayward” and “Richmond” permits belong to Hayward, Wisconsin and Richmond, Virginia.

Why this matters for Permit Watch

Permit Watch, our weekly list of commercial jobs for electrical, HVAC and refrigeration shops, only uses official records we can check every weekday. That's why it covers San Francisco, San José, unincorporated Marin County and Campbell today, and why we add a city when it publishes current data, not before. You can see when we last checked each source on our data status page.
