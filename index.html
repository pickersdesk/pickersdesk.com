The Turnover · for restaurant vendors

Bay Area restaurants and bars opening, closing and changing hands, from state liquor-license filings and city business registrations. New restaurants pick their equipment, suppliers and service companies before they open, and a filing usually shows up while those choices are still open.

  • Equipment dealers: they buy the kitchen before they open the doors.
  • Liquidators and used equipment: hear about closed kitchens before the auction listing goes up.
  • Service companies (refrigeration, hoods, install): new owners often bring in new vendors.
  • Distributors, linen, POS: win the account before the first delivery.

Permit Watch · for commercial trades

Commercial build-outs and tenant improvements, filtered to jobs a small shop can realistically bid: who's building, how big, the general contractor where the city lists one, and whether electrical and mechanical are still to be permitted.

How it works

From raw filing to your inbox.

  1. The agency files it

    Every business day, the state and cities post new license applications, transfers, closures and building permits.

  2. We pick it clean

    We capture every record daily, remove duplicates, sort each one into what it means for you, and put it in plain English.

  3. You get the short list

    One email a week: who, where, and what stage they're at. Facts from the record, no opinions.

Straight from the source

Every entry comes from a public filing and can be traced back to it. Nothing guessed, nothing made up.

Early, not late

Filings typically appear weeks to months before the grand-opening sign goes up, or before a closed kitchen is sold off.

Short on purpose

Built to read in two minutes on a phone between jobs.
