The problem it solves

By the time a tenant improvement shows up on a bid board, the general contractor already has a short list of subs. The earliest public sign is the building permit, but city permit systems are built for inspectors, not for sales. There are thousands of entries a month, most of them residential or too small or too big for a 3-to-30-person shop.

What we do

  • Capture everything daily. Every San Francisco and San José building permit, every business day, with status changes tracked.
  • Keep only your kind of job. Commercial and tenant-improvement work, roughly $150,000 to $2.5 million. No houses, no data centers.
  • Flag what matters to a sub. Whether electrical and mechanical are still to be permitted, which general contractor is on the job, kitchen and refrigeration work, and the earliest-stage filings.
  • Send a short weekly list. Plain English, one email, readable on a phone between jobs.

What's in every lead

  • Project and tenant, where listed
  • Street address and city
  • Estimated value and square footage
  • Scope: building, electrical, plumbing, mechanical
  • Stage: filed, issued, or trades still to be permitted
  • General contractor, where the city lists one (San José does; San Francisco doesn't)
  • Permit number, so you can check it at the source

How to use it

  • Trades open: call the general contractor or owner's rep before the electrical or mechanical permit is pulled.
  • GC named: get on that contractor's bid list for this job and the next one.
  • Just filed: these are the earliest stage; worth a note in your follow-up calendar.
  • Kitchen: refrigeration, hoods and walk-ins, often outside the main electrical scope.
